# Privacy Policy

_Last updated: 2026_

Knowly ("we", "the service") is a community question-and-answer platform. This
policy explains what personal data we collect, why we collect it, how long we
keep it, who we share it with, and the rights you have over it. By creating an
account you agree to the handling of data described here.

## 1. Data we collect

- **Account data** — the email address and username you register with, and a
  salted bcrypt hash of your password. If you sign in with Google, we store your
  Google account identifier and verified email instead of a password.
- **Profile data** — your optional biography and avatar image.
- **Content you create** — questions, answers, comments, votes, direct messages,
  friend relationships, file attachments, and your conversations with the AI
  assistant.
- **Technical data** — your IP address and basic request metadata are written to
  server logs for security, abuse prevention, and debugging.

## 2. How we use your data

- To authenticate you and keep you signed in (session cookies).
- To display your contributions and profile to other users.
- To operate platform features: notifications, search, messaging, friends, and
  the AI assistant.
- To screen content for policy compliance before it becomes visible to others
  (see Moderation, below).
- To secure the service and investigate abuse.

We do **not** sell your personal data, and we do **not** send marketing email.
All notifications are in-app only.

## 3. Third parties

Operating Knowly requires sharing limited data with the following processors:

- **Google (OAuth 2.0)** — if you choose "Sign in with Google", Google
  authenticates you and returns your account identifier and verified email.
- **The LLM provider** — when you use the AI assistant, the messages in that
  conversation (and, when you ask for help with a question, that question's title
  and body) are sent to our language-model provider to generate a response.
- **The moderation provider** — the text of every question, answer, and comment
  is sent to an automated moderation service to check it against our content
  policy before publication.

We do not control these providers' independent privacy practices; review their
policies for details.

## 4. Avatar images served as public static files

For performance, avatar images are served as **unauthenticated static files**
behind an unguessable (random UUID) URL. Anyone who knows the exact URL can
fetch the image, but the URLs are not listed and are not derivable from your
account. If you remove your avatar, the file is deleted from disk. This trade-off
is documented in our Known Limitations.

## 5. Data retention

- Content you post is retained while your account is active.
- Server logs are retained for a limited operational period.
- Old notifications and expired sessions are pruned periodically.

## 6. Your rights and choices

- **Access and update** — you can view and edit your profile, change your
  password, and manage your content at any time.
- **Account deletion (anonymization)** — see below.

## 7. Account deletion and anonymization

When you delete your account, we **anonymize** rather than hard-delete it, so the
discussions you contributed to remain coherent for the community. In a single
transaction we:

1. Replace your username with an anonymized stub (e.g. `[deleted_1a2b3c4d]`) and
   clear your email, password, biography, avatar, and any linked Google identity.
2. **Delete every session**, signing you out everywhere.
3. **Delete all of your direct messages**, on both sides of each conversation.
   This means the other participant also loses their copy — we prioritize the
   deleting user's privacy over the other party's retention.
4. Remove your identity from notifications generated by your actions.
5. **Delete your AI assistant conversations and their messages.**
6. **Delete all of your friendships**, so the anonymized account does not linger
   in anyone's friends list.
7. **Retain your questions, answers, and comments**, re-attributed to the
   anonymized stub. Your avatar file is removed from disk.

This is irreversible.

## 8. Security

Passwords are hashed with bcrypt. Session tokens are random values stored only as
SHA-256 hashes, so a database leak does not yield usable sessions. All external
traffic is served over HTTPS.
